## 1. Goal and desired conditions

Societies can prepare for shocks, keep essential services available and restore them fairly when they fail. People facing the greatest barriers can reach help. Institutions learn from disruptions and adapt when existing arrangements repeatedly cause harm.

## 2. What Natalie Weber means by societal resilience

The risk dossier brings together continuity of water, energy and other services, preparedness, local support networks and institutional learning. The terminology guide asks who can participate and who bears losses. The reference brings these aspects together while keeping social cohesion visible across resilience and inclusion.

## 3. Why the goal matters

Essential services share dependencies. A care facility may need electricity, water, communications and working transport to remain open. Preparing for a single failure leaves some of the largest risks untouched when several services depend on the same asset.

## 4. How failures can spread

In a heatwave, a power outage can interrupt cooling. Heat exposure and illness can increase, and emergency demand can crowd out other care. Recurring disruptions can drive staff or providers away, leaving the area less prepared for later events. Each step needs evidence. Backup capacity, neighbourhood assistance and timely repairs can interrupt the chain.

## 5. UN targets and the WÖk contribution

Targets 1.5, 11.5, 11.b and 13.1 cover vulnerability, disaster losses and preparedness. Indicators 1.5.1, 11.5.1 and 13.1.1 share the same measure of deaths, missing persons and directly affected people per 100,000 population. Indicator 11.5.3 tracks damage to critical infrastructure and service disruptions. WÖk asks who loses a specific service, what other failures follow and how recovery is tested. The dossier also calls for examining redundancy, social support and learning together. [UN indicator metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/)

## 6. People, planet and democracy

Floodplain protection can buffer flooding and sustain habitat. Service disruptions may still fall unevenly on residents with limited mobility or savings. Public decisions about where to protect, relocate or restore services affect exposure and the ability of residents to shape later decisions.

## 7. Relationships with other goals

Heat preparedness supports health under SDG 3 and safer settlements under SDG 11 if measures reach exposed people. Systemic cooperation helps service operators handle shared dependencies. Additional backup infrastructure can require land, materials and maintenance. The design and actual use determine the trade-off. [IPCC AR6, Cities, Settlements and Key Infrastructure](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-6/)

## 8. Application example

In a hypothetical town, several agencies use the same warning system. An exercise tests whether people receive and understand warnings when mobile service fails. The town compares delivery and response across neighbourhoods and repairs an inaccessible fallback route.

## 9. Observation and assessment

MasterItem `WOK-SYS-193` counts active, evaluated partnerships per year under register rule R20. This describes cooperation activity. Continuity of the local water service is a candidate state variable. Outage duration in hours per affected service area is one possible indicator. Outage logs record time, location and the population affected. Compare the observation with the baseline and verify restoration in a Reality Check. Both mappings remain open. [Master register v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

When an applicable hard protection limit for essential supply is established, savings elsewhere cannot offset a breach. The applicable method and the severity of the deficit determine how Reverse Merit Order affects the assessment.

## 11. How to achieve and sustain the goal

Map dependencies across providers, test alternative supply and communication routes, check who can use them, and assign responsibility for correcting failures exposed by exercises.

## 12. Evidence and limits

The WÖk dossier supplies the model and the Sendai framework supplies disaster monitoring definitions. A case-control study of the Chicago heatwave identified circumstances associated with deaths. Outcomes in other places and the effects of specific interventions require local evidence.
