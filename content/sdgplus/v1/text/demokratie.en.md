# Quality of democracy

## 1. The intended condition

People can influence public decisions, hold power to account and correct mistakes peacefully. A resident can learn what a council plans, give reasons for a different choice and follow the response. Elections, public input and independent review provide distinct routes to change.

## 2. What the WÖk means by it

The terminology guide asks who is represented, how people can take part and whether a decision can be challenged and corrected. The dossier includes fair elections, limits on power, peaceful transfers of power and protection for minorities and opposition. Decision-makers can hear residents who lack voting rights without changing the rules on who votes. [Terminology guide v1.8, section 4.2](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/) · [Risk and Resilience Dossier, section 7.3](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/sdgs-sdgplus-risiko-resilienzregister-systemresilienz/#7-3-sdg-plus-1-demokratiequalitaet)

## 3. Why it matters

Decisions about schools, flood protection and public spending distribute benefits and risks. Participation can reveal who would bear an overlooked cost. Published reasons enable scrutiny and an effective review process can make correction possible while options are still available.

## 4. What can happen when it falls short

If consultation excludes people who work shifts or need accessible formats, fewer of them can report hazards. A school can then be built on a site that leaves some children with unsafe routes. If complaints repeatedly produce no response, residents may participate less in later decisions. Land commitments can carry the original cost into the next generation. These are plausible paths in a hypothetical example. The OECD survey identifies an association between perceived political voice and trust in government. [OECD Trust Survey 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2026-results_9eb63fec-en/full-report/socioeconomic-conditions-political-agency-and-trust_bb385ad9.html)

## 5. What the UN covers and how the WÖk extends it

Paragraph 9 of the 2030 Agenda recognises democracy, good governance and the rule of law. Target 16.7 covers inclusive, responsive, participatory and representative decision-making. Indicator 16.7.1 compares representation in public institutions with the population, while 16.7.2 measures perceptions of inclusive and responsive decisions. The WÖk examines access, checks on power and evidence of correction in the particular procedure. Where material, it also examines manipulation risks and platform influence on that process. [2030 Agenda](https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda) · [UN indicator metadata for 16.7](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/?Goal=16&Target=16.7)

## 6. People, planet and democracy in one case

When a council plans flood protection, tenants and emergency services can identify overlooked dangers. A change to the plan can affect safety, water retention and which businesses remain accessible during severe weather. Concentrating damage in one district can feed back into participation. Those effects need evidence on exposure, design and outcomes.

## 7. Connections with other goals

SDG 11.3.2 concerns regular and democratic civil society participation structures in urban planning. A particular structure can support democratic quality if input reaches decision-makers in time. Media independence, effective legal remedies and public accountability help people follow decisions and exercise oversight. One documented change keeps one identity across these references. [UN metadata for 11.3](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/?Goal=11&Target=11.3)

## 8. Worked example

In a hypothetical school siting process, the council publishes its selection criteria before consultation and explains which proposed changes it accepted. A later review compares the plan with the actual routes available to children, including travel time and accessibility.

## 9. Observation, measurement and assessment

The WÖk sequence is MasterItem → StateVariable → Indicator → Observation → Analysis / Reality Check. Masterregister v1.5 links `WOK-SYS-158` to this field and records a generic democracy index on a 0–1 scale. Its state-variable and indicator mappings require review. A local study could instead define timely access to consultation information, specify the population, period and survey method, then examine whether submissions affected the decision. [MasterItems v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

In Germany, relevant legal safeguards include the democratic principle and federal electoral guarantees. When an applicable hard safeguard is breached, an environmental or financial benefit cannot offset that breach. Reverse Merit Order prioritises material deficiencies under the applicable WÖk method. Low turnout calls for investigation of barriers and causes. [Basic Law, articles 20 and 38](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html) · [Terminology guide v1.8](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/)

## 11. How to improve and maintain it

Authorities can publish alternatives early, provide accessible ways to comment and document their response to objections. A later Reality Check compares actual consequences with the decision’s assumptions and identifies who can act on the findings. [Learning democracy dossier v1.1](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/blog/lernfaehige-demokratie.html)

## 12. Evidence and limits

The WÖk guide sets the model’s terms. The UN metadata defines statistical measures. Acemoglu and colleagues examine regime transitions and GDP per capita, at national scale. That evidence cannot determine the effects of a specific council procedure. [UN indicator framework](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/indicators/indicators-list/) · [Acemoglu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1086/700936)
