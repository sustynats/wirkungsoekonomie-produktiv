## 1. Goal and desired conditions

People and organisations with different responsibilities coordinate their work on shared problems. They agree who contributes resources, who bears costs, how disputes are handled and how outcomes will be checked.

## 2. What Natalie Weber means by systemic cooperation

The risk dossier uses the term for cooperation across authorities, sectors and supply chains. It covers shared standards, coordination, fair contributions and learning. The later WÖk cooperation study develops proposals on enabling conditions and integrity. Those proposals remain distinct from the established field description.

## 3. Why the goal matters

Individual institutions can meet their own targets while the shared system deteriorates. A water authority, farms and housing planners may follow their own mandates yet collectively raise water demand above dependable supply. Coordination can make trade-offs and the distribution of costs visible early.

## 4. How failures can spread

Unclear responsibilities can leave part of a plan unimplemented. Providers may duplicate work or shift costs to less powerful partners. Repeated failures can weaken willingness to share data or invest in common capacity, leaving later decisions to a smaller group. Rules and resources need to fit the actual coordination problem.

## 5. UN targets and the WÖk contribution

Targets 17.14, 17.16 and 17.17 concern policy coherence and partnerships. Indicators cover countries with coherence mechanisms, progress reported in development effectiveness frameworks and financial commitments to infrastructure public-private partnerships. Target 6.5 applies integrated water resources management at sector level. WÖk examines responsibilities, actual delivery, cost distribution, feedback and changes in the system affected by one collaboration. ## 6. People, planet and democracy

An agreement across a river basin can link household supply, farming and habitat protection. Its rules affect who can draw water during drought and how environmental limits are enforced. Accessible decisions and independent review give affected communities ways to contest the rules and report shifts in burden.

## 7. Relationships with other goals

Shared monitoring can support SDGs 6 and 15 where the data cover the affected ecosystem and the agreement represents its interests. Societal resilience can benefit from mutual aid with tested spare capacity. A shared central system can also become a common point of failure. Public accountability helps establish who must act when commitments slip.

## 8. Application example

In a hypothetical catchment, municipalities and growers agree demand triggers, data sharing and a complaint route. A scheduled review compares water levels and restrictions across upstream and downstream areas. The group revises the agreement if vulnerable users bear disproportionate restrictions.

## 9. Observation and assessment

MasterItem `WOK-SYS-199` counts active, evaluated partnerships per year under rule R20. This can describe activity. For one partnership, define reliability of agreed contributions as the state variable, examine the share of due contributions fulfilled, and record effects on water availability separately. A Reality Check reviews alternatives and unintended effects. Case-specific mappings remain open. [Master register v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

Savings achieved through a joint supply-chain programme cannot offset an established breach of an applicable hard labour or rights limit. The specific method determines priority and consequences. Smaller coordination shortcomings require an ordinary assessment of materiality. Reverse Merit Order makes materially critical deficits visible and can limit the overall assessment under the applicable method.

## 11. How to achieve and sustain the goal

Set clear responsibilities, agreed rules for access and review, and a plan to measure outcomes. Check whether less powerful participants have practical influence and whether they can challenge unfair terms or leave.

## 12. Evidence and limits

Ostrom's comparative research on governing shared resources provides evidence about particular arrangements and their limits. Other sectors need their own evidence. The WÖk dossier and later study inform the model.
