## 1. Goal and desired conditions

People can use and question digital systems that affect them and access a practical alternative where available. Organisations can explain decisions, correct mistakes and maintain workable alternatives. Technology supports human capabilities while preserving rights and practical control.

## 2. What Natalie Weber means by technology and digital responsibility

The risk dossier addresses data governance, algorithmic responsibility, platform dependence and secure infrastructure. The terminology guide adds that digital self-determination requires real choices over data and actions. Digital self-determination remains visible under the broader heading.

## 3. Why the goal matters

Automated workflows can make the same mistake thousands of times before anyone can intervene. When a service depends on a single provider or inaccessible technical process, affected people can also lose the means to obtain a review or use a different route.

## 4. How failures can spread

Biased input or an unsuitable proxy may misclassify a person's needs. Repeated denials may shift cases to understaffed appeal channels. Low recorded demand can then justify smaller budgets for the same group. These mechanisms are hypotheses until examined in the system concerned. A US health-algorithm study illustrates how spending used as a proxy for need produced racial disparities in the studied setting. [Obermeyer et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax2342)

## 5. UN targets and the WÖk contribution

Targets 4.4, 9.c and 17.8 address skills, connectivity and technology use. Indicator 4.4.1 tracks specific ICT skills, 9.c.1 mobile-network coverage and 17.8.1 internet use. Target 16.10 covers information access and fundamental freedoms. WÖk examines whether users retain access, real choices and effective remedies in a concrete service. The dossier adds assessment of model fairness, reviewability, dependency and responsibility even where connectivity is good. [UN indicator metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/)

## 6. People, planet and democracy

A digital service can widen access and reduce some travel while raising energy demand or requiring new equipment. Conditions for public oversight change when a vendor controls the data or only the vendor can explain a decision. Compare material service, resource and oversight effects in the same implementation. [IEA, Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai)

## 7. Relationships with other goals

SDGs 4 and 9 support skills and access. Inclusive services also need support and accessible design. Public transparency can make automated decisions reviewable under SDG+ 9, while disclosure requires controls for personal data. Digital efficiency may support SDGs 12 and 13 depending on workload growth and electricity sources.

## 8. Application example

In a hypothetical housing service, applicants can challenge an automated rejection through a staffed channel. The operator checks whether affected applicants receive understandable reasons, can submit new evidence and get a corrected decision within the stated process.

## 9. Observation and assessment

Register item `WOK-G-230` specifies privacy incidents per million customers (R02). `WOK-G-231` specifies coverage of relevant features or products as a percentage (R25). Scope, reporting changes and missing incidents matter. For the housing case, observe the share of identified erroneous decisions that were corrected, with a denominator and time period, and revisit the reasons in a Reality Check. State-variable and indicator mappings require review. [Master register v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

A proven violation of an applicable hard privacy or rights limit stands independently of efficiency gains. Apply the governing legal requirements and the consequences specified for the relevant WÖk assessment. Reverse Merit Order makes materially critical deficits visible and can limit the overall assessment under the applicable method.

## 11. How to achieve and sustain the goal

Include accessible alternatives, data portability, incident review and workable exit terms in procurement. Use independent testing and complaints to locate failures, assign responsibility and verify corrections.

## 12. Evidence and limits

The dossier and terminology guide ground the model. The health study provides empirical evidence for one deployment setting. UN and Eurostat statistics provide distinct population-level access and skills measures.
