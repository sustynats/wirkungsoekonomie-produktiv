# Rule of law

## 1. The intended condition

Public power is subject to law, and people can obtain an independent review and effective remedy. Clear rules, protection of rights, fair procedures and enforceable decisions all contribute to that condition.

## 2. What the WÖk means by it

The terminology guide examines whether procedures work in practice, an independent body can review a decision and public authorities apply the law without arbitrariness. The dossier also describes legal certainty, protection of rights and anti-corruption. Rules, judicial independence and practical access to a remedy are distinct parts of the assessment. [Terminology guide v1.8, section 4.2](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/) · [Risk and Resilience Dossier, section 7.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/sdgs-sdgplus-risiko-resilienzregister-systemresilienz/#7-5-sdg-plus-3-rechtsstaatlichkeit)

## 3. Why it matters

People can exercise legal protections only if they can raise an issue, get a fair decision and secure a remedy. Predictable enforcement can also affect investment. A study of Brazilian bankruptcy reform found that differences in local court enforcement shaped changes in firms’ access to finance. This result concerns a specific reform and jurisdiction. [Ponticelli and Alencar, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjw015)

## 4. What can happen when it falls short

If notice of a permit is inaccessible, residents may lose their practical opportunity to challenge it. If unfair conditions persist, exposure and losses can accumulate while the parties with least access bear the greatest burden. Delayed remedies can also lock regulators and firms into costly decisions. These are plausible paths. Case records, exposure measurements and the timing of regulatory responses can help test them.

## 5. What the UN covers and how the WÖk extends it

Paragraph 9 of the 2030 Agenda names the rule of law. Target 16.3 promotes it and equal access to justice. Indicator 16.3.3 tracks whether people experiencing disputes in the previous two years accessed a formal or informal dispute resolution mechanism. This leaves independence, fairness and effective remedies to be assessed with additional evidence. The WÖk tests such conditions against the specific decision and applies relevant hard legal safeguards within its assessment procedure. [2030 Agenda](https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda) · [UN metadata for 16.3.3](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-16-03-03.pdf)

## 6. People, planet and democracy in one case

Independent review of an environmental permit can require an authority to re-examine health and pollution evidence. Corrective action could protect residents and a river while affecting a firm’s operating costs. Compliance measurements show whether conditions changed. Public access to reasons and decisions can support accountability.

## 7. Connections with other goals

SDG 16 also covers bribery and corruption under target 16.5. Impartial review can protect democratic participation and give people a way to demand correction when public accountability fails. Where applicable, the Aarhus Convention provides routes to environmental justice. Legal standing and time limits depend on the procedure. [UN SDG 16](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal16) · [Aarhus Convention](https://unece.org/environment-policy/public-participation/aarhus-convention/content)

## 8. Worked example

In a hypothetical permit case, a resident receives timely notice, accesses the supporting documents and seeks independent review. The decision identifies the evidence needed for a corrected permit, and the authority documents how it implements the remedy.

## 9. Observation, measurement and assessment

The WÖk sequence is MasterItem → StateVariable → Indicator → Observation → Analysis / Reality Check. MasterItem `WOK-SYS-176` uses a generic 0–1 index in v1.5. The links to a defined state variable and indicator require review. A case study could define access to review and track elapsed time, cost and proportion of eligible people securing a decision, with jurisdiction, population and period documented. Court statistics and surveys support different inquiries. ## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

In Germany, Article 19(4) of the Basic Law provides access to courts where public authority violates a person’s rights. Article 97 protects judicial independence. Benefits elsewhere cannot offset a verified breach of an applicable hard safeguard. Reverse Merit Order records and prioritises material deficiencies under the specific approved method. [Basic Law, articles 19 and 97](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html) · [Terminology guide v1.8](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/)

## 11. How to improve and maintain it

Authorities can provide clear notices, accessible filing routes and reasons with review options. Organisations can retain evidence for correction and document how they address complaints. An independent follow-up can examine whether remedies worked and whom remaining barriers affect.

## 12. Evidence and limits

The WÖk guide sets the model’s terms. The Venice Commission’s checklist gives institutional criteria, and court data describe practical operation. The Brazilian study tests a specific economic mechanism. Legal conclusions depend on the applicable jurisdiction, material facts and current law.
