## 1. Goal and desired conditions

People can access, contribute to and influence cultural and social life regardless of background or affiliation. Institutions address barriers to participation and protect the people and cultural expressions most at risk of exclusion.

## 2. What Natalie Weber means by cultural diversity and inclusion

The risk dossier covers cultural spaces, language, memory, accessibility and minority protection. The terminology guide links social cohesion to distribution and meaningful participation. Social cohesion also supports societal resilience. Here the focus is on who can belong, participate and shape decisions without giving up differences.

## 3. Why the goal matters

A cultural programme can draw large audiences while excluding residents who cannot afford it, reach it or participate in its language and format. Institutions that rarely hear from those residents have less evidence about barriers and may keep funding inaccessible services.

## 4. How failures can spread

An inaccessible venue first limits attendance. A sustained absence can restrict cultural networks or opportunities for contributors. Over time, usage data skewed by exclusion may reinforce funding decisions and narrow the range of programmes. Transport, fees, accessible formats and recruitment offer different ways to test that chain.

## 5. UN targets and the WÖk contribution

Targets 10.2, 10.3 and 16.b address inclusion and discrimination. Indicators 10.3.1 and 16.b.1 share a measure of self-reported discrimination or harassment in the previous 12 months. Target 11.4 protects cultural and natural heritage, and its indicator tracks per-capita expenditure. Target 4.7 includes appreciation of cultural diversity. WÖk checks who can actually participate and contribute locally, and adds review of cultural expression, accessibility and minority protection in context. [UN indicator metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/)

## 6. People, planet and democracy

A cultural space can foster participation and preserve community knowledge. In a heritage project that also touches land management, whose knowledge gets heard can influence ecological and access decisions. An environmental connection depends on the project and the decisions it affects.

## 7. Relationships with other goals

Education and transport under SDGs 4 and 11 can support participation if programmes and venues are usable. Accessible ways to contribute can help democracy quality when institutions consider the input. Social networks may mobilise help during a crisis while leaving people outside the network underserved.

## 8. Application example

A hypothetical city changes venue access, admission fees and interpretation provision in its public cultural programme. It compares participation and invitations to contribute by neighbourhood, while asking residents about barriers that may persist even when attendance rises.

## 9. Observation and assessment

Register item `WOK-RE-127` uses a transit score from 0 to 1 under rule R14, with the local data source still open. It can inform travel access. For a particular venue, define actual participation as a separate state variable, record attendance and evidence of opportunities to contribute, and check after changes against a baseline. State-variable and indicator mappings remain open. [Master register v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

High attendance cannot offset an established breach of an applicable hard non-discrimination or rights limit. Assess lawful choices about schedules and resources on their merits. Apply Reverse Merit Order under the relevant method and severity criteria.

## 11. How to achieve and sustain the goal

Budget for accessible design, involve people facing barriers in decisions and test changes under real conditions. Keep complaint and feedback routes open and report what changes in response.

## 12. Evidence and limits

A hiring field experiment tested one specific access barrier. It does not establish effects in every cultural setting. The UN disability convention supplies independent rights standards. UNESCO supplies culture measures, while the WÖk dossier defines the model field.
