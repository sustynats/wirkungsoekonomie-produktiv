# Quality of public discourse and debate

## 1. The intended condition

People can disagree, test one another’s reasons and correct errors without being pushed out. A useful discussion can reveal a problem, change a decision or make a continuing disagreement intelligible.

## 2. What the WÖk means by it

The terminology guide concerns access, source verification, openness to other perspectives, dissent and the ability to correct errors. The dossier treats public debate as part of society’s capacity to handle conflict. Assessment concerns the conditions and conduct of discussions, including barriers, intimidation and documented responses. Political agreement or the popularity of participants does not determine the result. [Terminology guide v1.8, section 4.2](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/) · [Risk and Resilience Dossier, section 7.6](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/sdgs-sdgplus-risiko-resilienzregister-systemresilienz/#7-6-sdg-plus-4-diskurs-und-debattenkultur)

## 3. Why it matters

Disagreements can contain information that decision-makers would otherwise miss. Explaining proposals, legal limits and points of uncertainty lets people challenge assumptions. A procedure that acknowledges conflicting interests can make even an unpopular decision open to review. In two US experiments, the topic of a cross-partisan conversation shaped whether it reduced affective polarisation in the short term. [Santoro and Broockman, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35731881/)

## 4. What can happen when it falls short

Threats can cause people with relevant experience to stop speaking. Decision-makers may then miss practical risks and make choices that deepen exclusion. Over time, affected groups may withdraw from future discussions, leaving even less information available. Trusted facilitation and accessible ways to respond may interrupt this hypothetical chain. In a US social media experiment, exposure to opposing political views sometimes increased polarisation. The result depends on the intervention, participants and platform. [Bail et al., 2018](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1804840115)

## 5. What the UN covers and how the WÖk extends it

Targets 16.7 and 16.10 concern participation and access to information. Target 4.7 includes education for a culture of peace, human rights and sustainability. Indicator 16.7.2 measures perceptions of inclusive and responsive decision-making, and 4.7.1 concerns how education systems incorporate specified topics. The WÖk assesses access, reason-giving, dissent and correction in a particular discussion. Its additional inquiry tracks material escalation risks and whether the discussion can feed back into decisions. [UN metadata for 16.7](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/?Goal=16&Target=16.7) · [UN metadata for 4.7](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/?Goal=4&Target=4.7)

## 6. People, planet and democracy in one case

In debate over a district heating project, residents can raise expected costs, installation disruption and neighbourhood needs. Evidence on actual bills, fuel use and emissions helps determine how the final design affects households and the environment. If officials explain how they used the evidence, people can identify what should be revisited.

## 7. Connections with other goals

Independent media can provide verifiable evidence. Democratic quality depends partly on whether dissent can reach those responsible for a decision. Systemic cooperation can use fair conflict resolution to overcome jurisdictional barriers. Moderation can protect access while also restricting expression if applied too broadly. Scope, reasons and appeal routes matter.

## 8. Worked example

In a hypothetical meeting on transport changes, a facilitator makes accessible formats available, records an objection about step-free access and asks planners to respond in writing. At the next meeting, participants can check whether the plan addresses the objection.

## 9. Observation, measurement and assessment

The WÖk sequence is MasterItem → StateVariable → Indicator → Observation → Analysis / Reality Check. `WOK-SYS-182` is labelled hate speech incidence in v1.5, with a generic index definition and open mappings. An observation plan needs a defined population, period and sampling procedure. Documenting access barriers and checking how specific proposals were handled can help assess a public procedure. A count of reported threats also depends on reporting access and willingness. Missing observations remain open. [MasterItems v1.5](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-master-items-register/)

## 10. Non-compensation and Reverse Merit Order

Freedom of expression and its legal limits are separate legal questions. A verified breach of a hard safeguard cannot be offset by a popular outcome or a well-attended meeting. The approved Reverse Merit Order method governs the significance and prioritisation of critical findings. Sharp disagreement alone does not establish a breach. [Basic Law, article 5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gg/art_5.html) · [Terminology guide v1.8](https://wirkungsoekonomie.de/bibliothek/woek-begriffsleitfaden-fuehrend/)

## 11. How to improve and maintain it

Organisers can publish evidence in accessible formats, provide several ways to comment and set clear moderation procedures. Decision-makers can explain how they dealt with objections. A Reality Check can compare those commitments with what actually happened.

## 12. Evidence and limits

The guide and dossier explain the WÖk expectations. UN statistics cover selected institutional conditions. The two studies concern different interventions, direct conversation and exposure on a social media platform. Neither establishes the outcome of a local public consultation. [UN indicator framework](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/indicators/indicators-list/) · [Bail et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1804840115)
